Why Do Cosine Absolute Scores Differ?

Understanding high-dimensional geometry vs. dense centroid clustering:

TF-IDF Score Scale (~0.12 - 0.18):

With 79,696 dimensions and an average query size of 25 terms, over 99.9% of coordinates are zero. Cosine scores are numerically modest, but rank separation is sharp and focused.

Word2Vec Score Scale (~0.80 - 0.93):

Averaging all 330 words in a review creates document vectors tightly clustered around a generic review centroid (words like film, scene, character). This elevates the base cosine angle without guaranteeing topical relevance.

Experimental Methodology & Findings

1. Pipeline Preprocessing

All reviews from the NLTK movie_reviews corpus underwent uniform cleaning: URL removal, lowercasing, non-alphanumeric stripping, tokenization, stopword removal, and WordNet lemmatization. Average review length: 331.6 tokens.

2. Why TF-IDF Won in Content Accuracy

When asked for an alien invasion movie, TF-IDF retrieved Independence Day and Aliens. Because rare terms like alien and invasion carry heavy inverse document frequency (IDF), they dominate the sparse dot product.

3. The Word2Vec Mean-Pooling Bottleneck

Word2Vec vectors capture brilliant word semantics (e.g., alien is adjacent to ripley, nostromo, and creature). However, unweighted mean pooling across 300+ words dilutes rare plot terms with generic commentary, causing general CGI essays to outrank specific plot matches.

4. Conclusion & Production Recommendation

For production IR systems, the recommended approach is a hybrid architecture: use BM25/TF-IDF for the initial high-precision candidate retrieval phase, followed by dense semantic embeddings for context-aware re-ranking.
